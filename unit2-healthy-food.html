3 · Reading

Leggi il testo.

What's on your plate?

Food gives us energy to study, play sport and grow. But which food is good for us, and how much of it do we need?

Many doctors use a simple picture: the healthy plate. Imagine your plate for lunch or dinner. Half of the plate is for vegetables and fruit: carrots, tomatoes, courgettes, apples, oranges… Try to eat food of different colours every day, because every colour gives your body something different.

A quarter of the plate is for bread, rice, cereal or potatoes. This food gives you energy for the whole morning or afternoon.

The last quarter is for fish, chicken, eggs, beans or nuts. Beans and nuts are a good choice because they come from plants. You don't need a lot of meat: two or three times a week is enough. Dairy products like milk, yoghurt and cheese are important too, because they are good for your bones and teeth.

What about drinks? The best drink is water: six to eight glasses a day is a good idea. Fizzy drinks and many fruit juices contain a lot of sugar, so they are not a good choice for every day.

Of course, nobody eats a perfect plate every day! A slice of cake at a birthday party or a few crisps with friends is not a problem. The problem starts when we eat too much sugar, too many chips and sausages, and not enough fruit and vegetables – every day, for months and years.

Remember: a healthy diet isn't about eating less. It's about eating well.

3 · Reading

Leggi il testo.

Don't throw it away!

Every day, people all over the world throw away a lot of food. According to the United Nations, people lose or waste about a third of all the food in the world, and a big part of this waste happens at home – not in shops or restaurants. At the same time, millions of people haven't got enough food to eat.

Why do we waste so much food? Often we buy too many things at the supermarket, or we cook too much rice and then throw away the food that's left. Sometimes we forget a carton of milk or a packet of ham at the back of the fridge, and then it's too late.

Here are a few simple ideas:

  • Before you go shopping, look in the fridge and write a list.
  • Read the label. "Best before" means that the food is at its best before that date, but you can often eat it after. "Use by" is about safety: don't eat the food after that date.
  • Don't cook too much. A cup of rice is enough for about three people.
  • Use old food in new recipes: old bananas, for example, are great in a cake.
  • At school or at a party, take only the food you can eat.

The 2030 Agenda of the United Nations has 17 goals for a better world. Goal 12 asks every country to cut food waste in half by 2030. Goal 3 is about good health for everyone. What we put on our plate – and in the bin – is connected to both.
